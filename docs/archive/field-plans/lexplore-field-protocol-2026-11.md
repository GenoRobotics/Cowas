2.	Experiment Design
Study Site: LéXPLORE Platform

A telephone call with the LéXPLORE team is proposed for 09:00 on Monday, 2 November, to assess weather conditions and confirm access. If conditions permit, a member of the platform team will provide an introduction to the facilities and equipment.

Two 24-hour sampling expeditions are planned for 2–3 November and 5–6 November 2026. Both expeditions are subject to suitable weather and confirmed access to the LéXPLORE platform.

Sampling is provisionally scheduled from 10:00 on the first day to 10:00 [5.1][6.1]on the following day. The start time must be confirmed once boat transfer, platform orientation, and equipment setup have been arranged.


The 2 m sample will represent near-surface water, while 20 m and 30 m[7.1] will provide intermediate and deeper comparison points. The 20 m depth targets the thermally variable intermediate region identified in LéXPLORE temperature profiles from 28–30 October 2026, allowing us to examine community variation near the shifting transition between warmer surface water and colder deeper water. The 30 m depth provides a colder comparison point that showed less temperature variability than 20 m over the same period.

3.1 Automatic Sampling
The regular sampling schedule will comprise 42 automatic water samples across two expeditions. Each expedition will include seven sampling events, with one sample collected at each of three provisional depths: 2, 20, and 30 m. An additional six automatic replicate samples are planned across the two expeditions, as described in Section 3.3. The final sampling depths will be confirmed before deployment.
COWAS will automatically filter each water sample through an individual Sterivex cartridge and add DNA Shield for preservation. Based on preliminary observations during testing, approximately 20 minutes will provisionally be allocated to each sample. 

Sampling event	Number of automatic samples	Approximate duration
One automatic sample at each of the three depths	3	1 hour
Two automatic samples at each depth, including one additional replicate	6	2 hours

Note: A manual change of the Sterivex cartridges or filter manifold may be required after approximately 14 automatic samples.[9.1][10.1]
3.2 Manual Sampling
A total of 12 primary manual samples will be collected across the two expeditions. During each expedition, manual samples will be collected at all three depths during two designated automatic sampling events, provisionally those beginning at 10:00 and 18:00.
Water will be collected using the Niskin bottle and electric winch available on the LéXPLORE platform. Each manual sample will be paired with the corresponding automatic sample from the same depth.
3.3 Replicate Sampling
An additional 12 replicate samples (6 automatic and 6 manual) will be collected to assess sampling consistency and reproducibility. Replicates will be independently collected under the same conditions and processed identically to primary samples.

4. Sample Collection Procedure
4.1 Preparation Before Sampling
1.	Confirm weather conditions, boat access, transport arrangements, and the time available for platform orientation and equipment setup. For 2 November, the weather and access call with the platform team is planned for 09:00.
2.	Confirm the first sampling start time after allowing sufficient time for installation and preparation. Update the sampling schedule if the proposed 10:00 start cannot be achieved.
3.	Prepare the Niskin bottle, electric winch, manual filtration equipment, and sample-handling materials.
4.	Prepare waterproof labels, sampling sheets, and a complete sample inventory covering primary samples, additional replicates, and field blanks.
5.	Prepare the sterile water and other materials required for the field blanks.
6.	Record the date, weather conditions, air temperature if available, personnel present, and relevant site observations.
7.	Retrieve the available LéXPLORE environmental profiles, particularly temperature, dissolved oxygen, and chlorophyll-a fluorescence. Record the dates and times of the profiles used to assess sampling conditions.
8.	Ensure that preservation supplies and transport arrangements are sufficient for the full deployment.
4.2 Preparation of the COWAS system
1.	Before deployment, clean the intake tubing, sampling pathways, manifold connections, adapters, and preservative delivery components according to the agreed COWAS preparation procedure. Félix's reference procedure uses 70% ethanol. Confirm completion of the cleaning procedure and removal of residual cleaning solution before sampling.
2.	Position COWAS securely on a stable support and install the agreed protection from weather and splashing.
3.	Connect the 24 V power supply and the laptop containing the COWAS operating code, using the required connection cable.
4.	Install unused Sterivex cartridges in the sampling manifold. Prepare a record linking each cartridge position to its sample ID, depth, sampling event, and replicate number.
5.	Install and secure the intake tubing. Verify the sampling depths and the arrangement used to select or access each depth. Install any prefilter included in the agreed configuration.[11.1][12.1]
6.	Fill the DNA Shield reservoir and prime the delivery line up to the check valve, following the COWAS preparation procedure.
7.	Programme the sample order, sampling times, flushing settings, filtration volume, and automatic DNA Shield addition. Allow approximately 20 minutes per water sample, based on preliminary testing, and confirm whether additional time is required for flushing, preservation, or depth changes.[13.1][14.1]
8.	Check cartridge capacity and preservative requirements for the full programme. Each expedition is planned to include 24 automatic environmental samples: 21 primary samples and three additional replicates. Field blanks must also be included when planning cartridge use and operating time.
9.	Schedule any required cartridge changes or preservative refills, and verify all connections and system settings before starting.
4.3 Automatic Sampling Procedure using COWAS
1.	Start the COWAS programme at the agreed time and record the actual programme start.
2.	Before each sample, ensure that the intake corresponds to the intended depth. Flush the relevant intake and sampling pathway using the agreed flushing procedure.
3.	Allow COWAS to filter water through the Sterivex cartridge assigned to that sample. Use the agreed target filtration volume and record the actual volume filtered where available.[15.1][16.1]
4.	Following filtration, COWAS will automatically add DNA Shield to preserve the sample. Record any reported failure or interruption affecting preservative delivery.
5.	Record the sample ID, depth, actual filtration start and end times, filtration volume, and any clogging, interruptions, or other deviations.
6.	During paired sampling events, coordinate each manual collection with the filtration interval of the corresponding automatic sample at the same depth.
7.	Check system operation and available error or diagnostic messages at the agreed monitoring times, including before the overnight sampling period.
8.	At a planned cartridge change or at the end of the deployment, verify which samples were completed before stopping the programme and handling the sampling manifold.
Any deviations from the planned schedule, including delayed or incomplete collections, should be documented.
4.4 Manual Sampling Procedure
1.	Prepare the manual sampling equipment before each designated paired sampling event, provisionally the events beginning at 10:00 and 18:00.
2.	Collect water from each selected depth using the Niskin bottle and electric winch, following the platform team's operating instructions and the agreed cleaning and handling procedure.
3.	Time each collection to coincide with the filtration interval of its corresponding automatic sample. Record the collection time, depth, and paired automatic sample ID.
4.	Transfer the water into an appropriately labelled sterile collection container or the agreed manual filtration setup.
5.	At designated replicate events, obtain an additional independent Niskin collection from the relevant depth and process it separately.
6.	Filter the manual sample as soon as possible after collection. Use a separate Sterivex cartridge for each sample and record the actual volume filtered and filtration start and end times.
7.	Add DNA Shield to the manual sample cartridge using the agreed manual preservation procedure.
8.	Record any differences between the manual and automatic methods, including prefiltration, filtered volume, delay before filtration, or preservation timing.
Manual and automatic samples should be collected as close together in time as possible to minimize environmental variation between paired samples.
4.5 Sample Filtration and Preservation
Based on Félix's protocol:[17.1]
Automatic water samples will be filtered through 0.22 µm PVDF Sterivex cartridges by COWAS. Following filtration, COWAS will automatically add DNA Shield to preserve the samples. Manual samples will be filtered separately using the same filter type, pore size, and target water volume wherever possible. DNA Shield will be added to manual samples following the agreed preservation procedure. Sterile handling procedures will be followed throughout filtration, cartridge recovery, and storage to minimize contamination.
At each scheduled manifold or cartridge change, and following completion of the final sampling sequence, the following steps will be performed:
1.	Check COWAS to confirm that all scheduled samples were collected successfully and review any error messages.
2.	Switch off COWAS and remove the sampling manifold.
3.	Retrieve the Sterivex cartridges and check that they are almost entirely filled with DNA Shield (approximately 2.2 mL).
4.	Securely screw caps onto both ends of each Sterivex cartridge.
5.	Place each cartridge in an individually labelled zip-lock bag.
6.	Verify that sample identifiers correspond to the sampling log.
7.	Store preserved samples at room temperature until transport to the laboratory.
4.6 Controls
●	2 COWAS field blanks: Process sterile water through COWAS tubing to assess contamination from the automatic sampling system
●	2 manual field blanks: Process sterile water using manual sampling equipment to assess contamination during collection and handling
●	2 extraction blanks: Process without environmental sample material to detect contamination (not collected during field work)
●	1 positive control: Process a known bacterial community 
4.7 Sample Identification >?
Label each environmental sample with a unique ID indicating the expedition, sampling date, scheduled sampling event, depth, method, and replicate number.
Example:
E1_20261102_1000_02M_AUTO_R1
This identifies expedition 1, 2 November 2026, the sampling event scheduled to begin at 10:00, the 2 m depth, automatic sampling, and the primary sample.
Use R1 for the primary sample and R2 for its designated additional replicate.
Maintain a sampling log recording sample ID, collection time, depth, water volume, filtration time, storage conditions, and any issues.
